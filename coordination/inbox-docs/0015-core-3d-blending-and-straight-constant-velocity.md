## Part A — 3D blending (`Core/Blending/BlendBuilder3D.cs`, `BlendSegment3D.cs`)

- `BuildBlendSegment(preceding, following, τ_start, τ_end, degree?, duration?, limits?)` (primary entry point) and the same with **absolute `Point3D`s**; `BuildBlendedSegmentPair` (both forms) returns the truncated neighbours and the blend. Windows: τ outside [0, 1] → `ArgumentOutOfRangeException`; empty window, window removing a whole neighbour (pair method), unsupported degree, non-positive prescribed duration → `ArgumentException`. Degree default as in 1D (neighbours' degree if equal, else 9).
- The boundary states are the neighbours' vector states at the two parameters (`PolynomialSegment3D.EvaluateState`); the coefficients come from `PolynomialSegment3D`, i.e. component-wise from the 1D machinery with the **common duration**.
- **Duration with limits**: the inherited duration (sum of the removed portions, floor 0.01 s) is kept if the 3D blend passes `VerifyLimits` on all three axes (new overload `ExtremaAnalyzer.VerifyLimits(PolynomialSegment3D, limits)`), else the shortest feasible duration; a prescribed duration is only verified (`KinematicLimitsViolatedException`); the limits passed in are the blend's limits (the caller takes `KinematicLimits.MostRestrictive` of the neighbours, as the 1D App does). `BlendSegment3D` exposes `InheritedDuration`, `Duration`, `AdjustedByLimits` like `BlendSegment1D`.
- **Multi-component feasibility without duplicating the search**: `ShortestFeasibleDuration.Find(degree, IReadOnlyList<State1D> starts, ends, limits)`. All components at rest → the same closed form with the **largest distance** (each component's T* is increasing in its distance, so the maximum is the largest); otherwise the same geometric-growth-plus-bisection search, where F(T) is the maximum over components and active limits and the lower bound is the maximum over components. The single-component `Find`, `Search`, `Feasibility` are now one-element calls of it (all earlier tests unchanged and green).
- **Absolute points**: `TauForPoint(segment, point, label)` scans the squared distance on 400 intervals, then refines by bisection on the root of d/dτ |p(τ) − q|² = 0 around the best sample (machine-precision parameter, not the ~1e-8 of a minimiser of the distance itself). If the distance at the optimum exceeds 1e-9 × the segment's extent (diagonal of the box that contains the path, `BlendBuilder3D.Extent`) → `ArgumentException` naming the label (blend start/end) and the distance. The result is clamped to [0.001, 0.999] like the 1D parameter. If the path comes close to the point at several places the globally closest sample wins (the first on a tie), unlike the 1D "first crossing"; an intended point on a self-crossing path is ambiguous (as the theory says: "straightforward only when the correspondence is unique").

## Part B — 3D constant velocity (`Core/ConstantVelocity/ConstantVelocityTrajectory3D.cs`)

- `new ConstantVelocityTrajectory3D(startState, endState, cvStart, cvEnd, speed, degree, limits, entryDuration, exitDuration, totalDuration)`: stretch from `cvStart` to `cvEnd` at `speed` (T_cv = ‖Δp‖/v, velocity vector v·n), entry/exit transitions as ordinary `PolynomialSegment3D`s with vector boundary states `(p, v·n, 0, 0, 0)` and a common duration; `ConstantVelocity` is a `LinearConstantVelocitySegment3D` (exactly linear, also as a degree-3 `PolynomialSegment3D`). `EvaluateState`, `GetAllSegments`, `TotalDuration`, `ConstantSpeed`, `Direction`.
- **Rules 1–3 are the 1D rules, shared, not copied**: the logic of the 1D constructor (prescribed durations win, remainder rules, proportional split, "total too short with limits" error) moved into the internal `TransitionDurationRules.Resolve`, which both `ConstantVelocityTrajectory1D` and `…3D` call with their own "weight" (Rule 1: shortest feasible, multi-component in 3D; Rule 2: `MeanVelocityDuration` with the Euclidean distance and the norms ‖v_b‖, v_cv). The 1D class lost its private copy; its 70+ tests pass unchanged.
- **Speed vs the velocity limit**: checked per axis, |v_cv n_i| ≤ v_max for x, y, z; explicit `KinematicLimitsViolatedException` naming the axis otherwise. (So a speed above v_max is accepted if no component exceeds it, which is the component-wise reading of the theory.)
- **Straight subpath**: `ConstantVelocityTrajectory3D.OnSegment(segment, τ_start, τ_end, …)` (and with absolute points) calls `StraightSubpath.Require`: 201 uniform samples between the two parameters must lie within 1e-9 × chord length of the chord and their projection must stay inside it (the path must not double back). Otherwise `NotSupportedException` whose message says that constant velocity along a curved subpath is **not implemented yet** and that arc-length reparameterization is still to be written, with the measured deviation. `IsStraight(...)` is public to test or ask without an exception. The constructor taking two points does no check (nothing underneath).
- Same exceptions as 1D (`KinematicLimitsViolatedException`, `DurationSearchFailedException` with the "entry/exit transition" prefix, `ArgumentException` for degenerate input: equal points, non-positive speed, total not above T_cv, total with both transition durations).

Test-design remark (not a code problem): a velocity limit just below the blend's peak is not always satisfiable for any duration, because the boundary velocities are fixed and a longer blend must still turn around (the search then reports `DurationSearchFailedException`, which is the correct answer); the test looks for a (window, limit) pair that is feasible, as the 1D one does.

## For the author (educational)

- **Why component-wise computation with a common duration is the vector blend.** A polynomial vector trajectory p(t) = (x(t), y(t), z(t)) is, by definition, three scalar polynomials; the boundary conditions on p are boundary conditions on each component, and the cost/constraint structure of the blend (matching position, velocity … at two instants) does not couple the components, so solving each axis separately gives exactly the vector solution. The only thing the axes must share is the **time**: the blend starts and ends at the same two instants on all axes, so one duration T_b serves all three. That shared T_b is the whole coupling, and it is why the limit handling asks for "the shortest T_b at which *all* axes are feasible" instead of three independent durations.
- **Why a straight subpath is exactly the straight-chord construction.** If the preserved subpath is a segment of a line, the point is p(s) = p₀ + s·n with s the distance along the line, so the motion is a one-dimensional motion along the direction n. A constant speed v along the path means ṡ = v, i.e. p(t) = p₀ + v t n: the chord traversed at constant velocity vector v·n. The "path-preserving, constant-speed" law of the theory therefore *is* the straight-chord law there, not an approximation of it; and because the transitions only need the endpoint positions and the velocity vector v·n, they are ordinary segments. The straight check (every sample on the chord, no doubling back) is what guarantees that "the preserved subpath" and "the chord" are the same set of points.
- **Why a curved subpath needs a different time law.** On a curve the speed is |ṗ| = |p′(u)|·u̇ for a path parameter u, and holding it constant means u̇ = v/|p′(u)|: the parameter must move *non-uniformly in time* so that arc length grows linearly. The arc length s(u) = ∫|p′(u)| du involves a square root of a polynomial, and its inverse u(s) is not a polynomial; so x(t) = p(u(t)) is not a polynomial in t. The polynomial-segment machinery of the project (coefficients, extrema, limits by polynomial peaks) cannot represent it exactly, which is why that case needs a dedicated numerical design (sampled reparameterization or an approximation with a stated error) and theory first.

## Discrepancies between theory and code

None for the formulation. Two readings I had to choose, both reversible: (1) the per-axis speed check (components, not the norm) — the request says "component-wise"; (2) the closest-point rule for absolute points (global minimum of the distance) versus the 1D "first crossing".

## Open questions

1. A 3D blend of two neighbours of **different** degrees uses degree 9 (rule of 1D); no test covers a mixed pair in 3D beyond `DefaultBlendDegree`.
2. `StraightSubpath` samples 201 points: a polynomial path could in principle leave the chord between samples (only for polynomials of degree 9 with fine oscillation). A rigorous check (the perpendicular components are polynomials: their extrema over [τ_s, τ_e] with `ExtremaAnalyzer`) is possible and cheap; I kept the sampled test because the tolerance is tight and it is simple. Say if you want the rigorous one.
3. Tolerance 1e-9 relative to the chord for straightness and 1e-9 relative to the extent for absolute points are my values ("a stated tolerance"); they are constants/parameters.
4. A `Trajectory3D`-level builder (add a 3D blend between two indices, like `TrajectoryBuilder1D.AddBlendBetween`) and the App wiring are not done (out of scope; R10b).
